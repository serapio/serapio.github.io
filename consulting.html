01

What I help with

Most teams do not need another dashboard. They need to know which evidence to trust, and what to do next.

Design

Evaluation that matches the decision

Offline and online evaluation, automated and human judgment, metrics tied to product behavior rather than convenience. Designed for the decision you actually have to make.

Audit

An outside read on existing evidence

Review of evaluation sets, metrics, annotation guidelines, and reported results. I look for coverage gaps, leakage, optimistic baselines, and conclusions the data cannot support.

Diagnosis

Failure analysis that finds the pattern

Error taxonomies, slice analysis, and multilingual comparison. The goal is to move from “it is bad on Spanish” to a specific, reproducible account of what is failing and why.

Data

Multilingual data and annotation review

Data acquisition, synthesis, annotation, and quality control across 12+ languages. I can review guidelines, sample quality, measure agreement, and help a pipeline survive contact with real speakers.

Infrastructure

Evaluation harnesses and observability

Reproducible eval hooks, traces, and reporting for RAG, dialogue, ASR, and classifier systems. Evaluation should be part of the product, not a notebook somebody runs before launch.

Readiness

Responsible AI and audit readiness

Human-in-the-loop, PII-respecting review processes; failure documentation; and the kind of evidence a serious reviewer will ask for. Not a compliance certificate, but a better position from which to earn one—especially in healthcare, where trust and compliance both depend on it.

02

How an engagement works

Usually a short, bounded piece of work with a written result. No mystery, no sprawling transformation program.

01

Scope the decision

What are you trying to decide: ship, fix, buy, explain, or stop? We agree on the question, the system boundary, and what evidence would count as an answer.

02

Gather evidence

I review existing evaluations, data, logs, and failure reports, then run targeted probes where the record is thin. Small and surgical beats large and theatrical.

03

Write it down

You get findings, a failure taxonomy, prioritized recommendations, and enough methodological detail to reproduce the work. Plain language, including the awkward parts.

04

Stay useful

A readout with the team, a review of proposed fixes, or a re-evaluation after changes. The point is not the report; it is a better decision.

03

Where I fit

I work best as an independent evaluator, not as a permanent member of the team or a vendor with a model to sell.

Good fit

  • You are shipping an ASR, NLU, LLM, or RAG feature.
  • Your system is multilingual, or about to be.
  • You have evaluations, but do not fully trust them.
  • You need an outside read before a launch or audit.
  • You are building for healthcare, where trust and compliance both matter.
  • Your data or annotation quality is an open question.
  • You want a written, reproducible account of failure.

Less good fit

  • You only need a leaderboard score on a public benchmark.
  • You want a rubber stamp for a decision already made.
  • You need long-term staff augmentation.
  • You need legal or formal compliance certification.
  • You want a model vendor and an evaluator in one invoice.
  • You are not prepared to look closely at the failures.
